The ones you called out

01–06

Reading “tamanax rex” as Tetrapanax ’Rex’ — it sits with tree ferns in the exotic bay, and ’Rex’ is the actual cultivar. Tamarix is in too, at 14, because it earns a place on its own.

  1. 01

    Tetrapanax papyrifer 'Rex'

    Rice-paper plant

    The cult exotic. Lobed leaves past 60cm on a suckering stem. Root-hardy in mild gardens, cut back by frost — and it suckers a long way. That's the sentence to say at the till, not the leaf size.

    SuckersExoticRoot-hardy
  2. 02

    Dicksonia antarctica

    Soft tree fern

    Sold by trunk height, and it puts on only 2.5–5cm a year — which is the whole answer to “why does it cost that”. Water into the crown, not the soil. Wrap the crown over winter.

    CITES App. IICrown-waterSlow
  3. 03

    Rhus typhina

    Stag's horn sumach

    Velvety antler stems and the best scarlet autumn in the trade. It also suckers aggressively — the single most important thing to say before it's sold. ’Dissecta’ gives a finer, ferny leaf. Sap irritates.

    SuckersAutumn starSap irritant
  4. 04

    Rodgersia podophylla 'Bronze Peacock'

    Your bronze peacock

    Bronze palmate leaves like a horse chestnut's, holding colour all summer, cream plumes above. Moist shade or a pond margin — it crisps in dry sun. Enormous texture for very little money.

    Moist shadeBold foliage
  5. 05

    Rhododendron 'Homebush'

    Deciduous azalea — the pink one

    Tight rounded rose-pink semi-double trusses. Knap Hill hybrid, AGM. Deciduous azaleas are the real knowledge gap — they colour up in autumn as well as flower, and take more sun than the evergreen kinds.

    AGMEricaceous2nd in genus
  6. 06

    Rhododendron luteum

    Yellow azalea

    Same gap, other half of the pitch: this is the scented deciduous azalea, honeysuckle-strong, AGM. ’Homebush’ sells on colour, this one sells itself before you've seen it.

    AGMScentEricaceous

Architectural & exotic

07–13

The deck already leans this way — Musa, Gunnera, Fatsia, Cordyline, Yucca — so these compound rather than dilute.

  1. 07

    Trachycarpus fortunei

    Chusan palm

    The hardiest palm you can honestly sell for a UK garden, genuinely H5. The answer when someone asks for a palm that will actually survive.

    H5 palmEvergreen
  2. 08

    Chamaerops humilis

    Dwarf fan palm

    The other hardy palm — clump-forming, smaller, spiny leaf stalks so keep it off paths. You already have a photo of this staged in the repo with no card built on it. Cheapest win on the list.

    Photo readySpines
  3. 09

    Melianthus major

    Honey bush

    Blue-grey saw-toothed leaves, and the foliage smells of peanut butter when you brush it. A guaranteed “smell this” moment on the bench.

    Scent trickTender-ish
  4. 10

    Astelia chathamica 'Silver Spear'

    Silver spear

    Metallic silver swords, coastal-tough, evergreen. Reads expensive on a bench. Wants sharp drainage or it rots at the base.

    CoastalSharp drainage
  5. 11

    Fargesia rufa

    Umbrella bamboo

    Clump-forming, not running. Probably the most useful single fact in the whole bamboo bay: this one stays put, Phyllostachys does not.

    ClumpingScreening
  6. 12

    Phyllostachys nigra

    Black bamboo

    Sell it, and sell a root barrier with it. Canes mature to black in their second or third year — not in the pot, which is worth saying before someone feels short-changed.

    RunsNeeds barrier
  7. 13

    Arbutus unedo

    Strawberry tree

    Evergreen, and it carries flowers and last year's fruit at the same time. In the heather family yet lime-tolerant, which catches people out.

    Lime OKEvergreenEdible fruit

Shrubs — the backbone

14–30

Where the money is. Several of these are things a new member of staff gets asked about on their first weekend.

  1. 14

    Tamarix ramosissima 'Pink Cascade'

    Tamarisk

    One of the very few genuinely salt-tolerant shrubs — a real answer for a seaside garden. Feathery pink plumes late summer. Prune hard or it goes bare-legged.

    CoastalPrune hard
  2. 15

    Pieris japonica 'Forest Flame'

    Lily-of-the-valley shrub

    Brilliant red new growth, then cream flower panicles. Ericaceous — far and away the most common reason it dies in a customer's garden.

    EricaceousShelter
  3. 16

    Skimmia japonica 'Rubella'

    Skimmia

    The winter container plant. It's male — red buds all winter, but no berries. Berries mean a female (’Nymans’) plus a male. Ericaceous, and it hates drying out.

    Winter sellerMale — no berriesEricaceous
  4. 17

    Ceanothus 'Concha'

    Californian lilac

    The most intense blue in the trade. Say the quiet part too: short-lived, and it resents cutting into old wood. Shelter and sharp drainage.

    Short-livedDon't hard-prune
  5. 18

    Physocarpus opulifolius 'Diabolo'

    Ninebark

    Near-black foliage, pink-white flowers, tough as old boots. The dark-leaved shrub for people who have already killed a Cotinus.

    HardyDark foliage
  6. 19

    Sambucus nigra 'Black Lace'

    Black elder

    Black lace-cut leaf and lemony pink flowers. Coppice in spring for the best foliage. Reads like an acer, grows like an elder — which is to say, anywhere.

    CoppiceEdible flower
  7. 20

    Syringa meyeri 'Palibin'

    Dwarf lilac

    Lilac scent on something that genuinely suits a container. The answer to “I want lilac but not a six-metre one”.

    ScentContainer
  8. 21

    Daphne bholua 'Jacqueline Postill'

    Daphne

    Arguably the best scent of any hardy shrub, in midwinter. And the rule that matters: Daphne resents disturbance. Plant once, never move it, don't dig near it.

    Best scentNever moveToxic berries
  9. 22

    Sarcococca confusa

    Christmas box

    Powerful winter scent from flowers you can barely see, and it thrives in dry shade — the hardest brief anyone brings into a garden centre.

    Dry shadeWinter scent
  10. 23

    Chaenomeles × superba 'Crimson and Gold'

    Flowering quince

    Flowers on bare wood in late winter, then quinces for jelly. Wall-trainable, thorny, effectively indestructible.

    ThornyEdible fruitWall shrub
  11. 24

    Kolkwitzia amabilis 'Pink Cloud'

    Beauty bush

    Arching sprays smothered in pink bells in June. An old-fashioned plant that has quietly gone out of fashion and shouldn't have.

    AGMPollinators
  12. 25

    Deutzia gracilis 'Nikko'

    Deutzia

    Low mounding habit, drowning in white stars in late spring. Genuinely low-maintenance and badly under-sold.

    Low maintenanceFront of border
  13. 26

    Ribes sanguineum 'King Edward VII'

    Flowering currant

    One of the earliest nectar sources for emerging bumblebees. The foliage is pungent — some people love it, some really don't; worth a sniff before buying.

    Early nectarScented foliage
  14. 27

    Aucuba japonica 'Crotonifolia'

    Spotted laurel

    Gold-splashed evergreen that will grow in deep dry shade under trees where nothing else will. Female plants berry if there's a male nearby.

    Deep shadeDioecious
  15. 28

    Kalmia latifolia 'Ostbo Red'

    Calico bush

    Crimped red buds opening to pink cups — and the flowers have a spring-loaded pollination trigger you can set off with a pencil. Ericaceous, slow, expensive, worth it.

    Demo-ableEricaceousSlow
  16. 29

    Enkianthus campanulatus

    Redvein enkianthus

    Tiered branches, small waxy bells, then one of the best autumn colours of any shrub. A connoisseur's plant that sells itself every October.

    Autumn starEricaceous
  17. 30

    Fothergilla major

    Witch alder

    Bottlebrush white flowers before the leaves, then multi-coloured autumn on one plant. Ericaceous, compact, criminally under-known.

    Autumn starEricaceous

Trees & small trees

31–40
  1. 31

    Betula utilis var. jacquemontii 'Doorenbos'

    Himalayan birch

    The whitest bark of any tree. Bark whitens with age — a young plant in a pot isn't a con, it just hasn't got there yet. Wash the trunk to keep it bright.

    Winter barkMulti-stem
  2. 32

    Amelanchier lamarckii

    Snowy mespilus

    Arguably the best small garden tree there is: white blossom, bronze new leaf, edible berries, fiery autumn. Four seasons on one plant, and it's cheap.

    Four-seasonEdibleWildlife
  3. 33

    Parrotia persica

    Persian ironwood

    A witch-hazel relative — spidery red flowers on bare wood, then spectacular autumn. Lime-tolerant, unlike most autumn-colour stars, which makes it a rescue recommendation on chalk.

    Lime OKAutumn star
  4. 34

    Nyssa sylvatica 'Wisley Bonfire'

    Tupelo

    Autumn colour that stops traffic. Needs moist, acid, lime-free ground — say it, or it comes back as a complaint.

    Acid onlyAutumn star
  5. 35

    Acer griseum

    Paperbark maple

    Cinnamon bark peeling in translucent curls, best with low winter sun behind it. Slow — which is exactly why it's priced where it is.

    Winter barkSlow2nd in genus
  6. 36

    Corylus avellana 'Contorta'

    Corkscrew hazel

    Twisted stems for winter structure and flower arranging, catkins in February. Usually grafted — pull off straight suckers from the base or it reverts to a plain hazel.

    GraftedCut stems
  7. 37

    Malus 'John Downie'

    Crab apple

    AGM, and the crab apple for jelly — larger conical orange-red fruit with real flavour, where most crabs are ornamental only. White blossom from pink buds; narrow when young, broadening with age. Also a good pollination partner for eating apples, which is the angle most people miss.

    AGMBest for jellyPollinator
  8. 38

    Koelreuteria paniculata

    Golden rain tree

    Yellow panicles in high summer when almost nothing else is flowering, then inflated papery lanterns. Wants a hot, dry spot.

    Summer flowerDrought
  9. 39

    Gleditsia triacanthos 'Sunburst'

    Honey locust

    Golden ferny foliage casting dappled shade light enough to plant underneath. Thornless form, shrugs off urban air.

    ThornlessUrban
  10. 40

    Eucalyptus gunnii 'Azura'

    Cider gum

    A compact cider gum bred for gardens rather than forestry. Silver-blue, evergreen, and a cut-foliage crop in its own right. Coppice to keep the round juvenile leaves.

    Compact formCut foliage

Climbers

41–45
  1. 41

    Wisteria floribunda 'Multijuga'

    Japanese wisteria

    Buy grafted, never seed-raised — a seedling can take twenty years to flower. Two prunes: July back to five buds, February back to two or three. The most valuable knowledge in the climber bay.

    Grafted onlyTwo prunesToxic seed
  2. 42

    Trachelospermum jasminoides

    Star jasmine

    Evergreen, glossy, powerfully scented white pinwheels, foliage bronzing in cold. The default answer for an evergreen scented climber — and already flagged as a photo gap in your notes.

    Evergreen scentKnown gap
  3. 43

    Hedera colchica 'Sulphur Heart'

    Persian ivy

    Huge gold-splashed leaves, self-clinging, shade-proof. Ivy's reputation is a lot worse than the plant, and late ivy flowers are a critical autumn nectar source.

    Self-clingingLate nectar
  4. 44

    Akebia quinata

    Chocolate vine

    Vanilla-and-chocolate-scented maroon flowers in spring. Needs two plants to set fruit, and the fruit is strange enough to be worth the trouble.

    ScentTwo for fruitVigorous
  5. 45

    Lonicera periclymenum 'Serotina'

    Late Dutch honeysuckle

    Scent is strongest at dusk, because it's moth-pollinated. One of those facts that explains the whole plant the moment you say it.

    Evening scentMoths2nd in genus

Small hardy plants

46–50

The Bridge Nursery end of the bench — small, hardy, bought in threes. Five here; the rest are on the reserve list.

  1. 46

    Geranium 'Rozanne'

    Cranesbill

    The best-selling hardy perennial there is — RHS Plant of the Centenary. June to first frost, and it's sterile, so it never stops trying to set seed. If one perennial belongs in this deck, it's this one.

    Best-sellerPlant of the Centenary
  2. 47

    Erysimum 'Bowles's Mauve'

    Perennial wallflower

    Flowers about ten months of the year. Also short-lived, two or three years — take cuttings. Say that at the point of sale and nobody minds; discover it later and they do.

    Flowers 10 monthsShort-lived
  3. 48

    Nepeta racemosa 'Walker's Low'

    Catmint

    Bees adore it and cats roll on it. The Chelsea chop in late May is the difference between a neat mound and a splayed mess by July.

    PollinatorsChelsea chop
  4. 49

    Helleborus × hybridus 'Anna's Red'

    Hellebore

    Flowers in winter, in shade. Cut the old leaves off in January — it reveals the flowers and takes leaf-spot away with them.

    Winter flowerCut old leavesToxic
  5. 50

    Hakonechloa macra 'Aureola'

    Japanese forest grass

    AGM. The one ornamental grass that genuinely prefers shade and moisture, flowing like water over the edge of a pot. Deciduous and very late up in spring — warn people before they dig it up thinking it's died.

    AGMShade grassLate up
