Plate 01

The deck as light

129 hue values

Every card stores a hue, 0–360, which drives its colour throughout the app. Read end to end, the deck has a signature: it is overwhelmingly a pink-to-magenta and green collection, because that is what a UK garden centre actually sells. The gaps are as informative as the clusters — almost nothing sits in the blues.

Plate 02

Hardiness, and the long tail of genera

135 cards

The deck sits where British gardening sits: H5 and H6 carry 87 of 135 cards — hardy through most of the UK. Only five cards are H2 or below, which is honest for a range built around what survives outdoors here.

Hardiness distribution

H1b–H7. The single H1b is the moth orchid — the only true houseplant in the deck.

Deepest genera

82 of 100 genera hold a single card. A very wide, very shallow collection — which is right for learning breadth, and worth knowing before anyone tries to teach a genus in depth.

Plate 03

What is actually filled in

the finding

This is the plate that matters. The learning layer is essentially complete — every card carries its ratings. The trade layer is all but empty: the card back sells itself on "buyer data is your own portfolio", and 3 of 135 cards have a trade price, a retail price or a margin. The feature works; the data was never entered.

Learning layer · ratings

seasonalImpact is the one blank column — defined, validated, rendered as nothing, rated on no card at all.

Trade layer · commercial fields

Peak season and cultivars are populated because the learning side needs them. Everything a buyer would use sits at 3.

Plate 04

How a plant becomes a card

the pipeline

Seven steps, four of them refusals. The design principle running through it: a tool that cannot be sure stops rather than guesses.

1

Research into the locked schema

PLANT-BRIEF.md produces a JSON in the exact incoming shape. Sources and uncertainties are recorded with it, not discarded.

2

Validate — refuses bad data

check-plant-json.js rejects a light level given as a compass facing, soil text over the measured panel width, a rating out of range. It never fills a blank in.

3

Photo staged, row inserted

Processed to 1200px under photos/<latin-slug>.jpg, then a row appended before the PLANTS:END marker.

4

Data gate — 17 seconds

Consistency, self-contradiction, provenance, then the whole deck audited as rendered. --quick stops here, which is what makes a 20-plant batch practical.

5

plants.csv re-exported

Both blocks, dealt and held. It used to drift on every insert because nothing wrote it back.

6

Full suite — 2m41s

Nine browser suites, three at a time. run-all.js --jobs 3.

7

Deploy, then verify the served bytes

Pages gates on the build stamp and the data audit, publishes, then fetches the live page and compares it to the commit. The stamp carries a hash of the file's own content, so it cannot lie about which build a phone is running.

Plate 06

Where the weight is

250 files · 91.1 MB

The app is one file of 5,149 lines — deliberately, because it ships as an installable page and an offline artifact. Almost all the repository's mass is photographs.

Bytes by directory

78% of the repo is photos. Worth knowing before anyone worries about the size of the code.

Code and prose

14 tools, 8 test suites, 15 markdown documents. The documentation is load-bearing: the protocol files are what stop a fix being undone later.
