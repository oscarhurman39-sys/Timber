Our mission

Learn plants,

Timber teaches you the plants you see on a garden-centre bench: what each one looks like, where it grows, how much water it wants, and whether it is safe. It does that with a deck of cards you swipe through. That is the whole app.

Why it exists

Plant knowledge should be easy to pick up and hard to lose.

I work on the floor of a garden centre in the UK. Every day customers ask the same kinds of questions: Will this survive the winter? How much sun does it need? Is it safe with a dog? What is that one with the red stems? The answers exist, but they live in books, on labels, and in other people's heads. Nobody has time to look them up while a customer is standing there.

Timber is my answer. Each plant is one card. Each card carries the handful of facts that actually matter at the point of sale, written to be read in ten seconds. Swiping through the deck a few minutes a day is how I learned the range myself, and it is how I want to help new staff, keen gardeners and anyone curious about plants do the same.

What we are trying to do

  • Educate. Give people accurate, plain-language plant information they can remember.
  • Respect the facts. Hardiness uses the Royal Horticultural Society's H-rating scale. A blank field means "not researched yet", never a guess.
  • Keep safety visible. Where a plant is harmful if eaten, a skin irritant, or subject to legal controls, the card says so on its own panel.
  • Stay simple. No feeds, no gamified nagging, no upsells. A deck of cards, a quiz, and a search box.
What the app is

A swipe deck of useful cards. Nothing more.

If you have used a dating app you already know how Timber works. The difference is that every card is a plant, and swiping right means "I know this one now".

→

Swipe right

Mark a plant as learned. It gets a gold stamp and joins your learned count.

←

Swipe left

Skip it for now. It comes back sooner in your review schedule.

↻

Double-tap

Flip the card over for the longer notes: pruning, uses, safety, and where the hardiness rating came from.

Also inside

  • Quiz mode. Short rounds built from the cards you are weakest on.
  • Review due. Simple spaced repetition: a plant you learned comes back for review after 1, 3, 7, 16 and 35 days.
  • Search. Common name, botanical name or use, and it forgives typos.
  • Show customer. A big plain-language view of one plant, safe to hand across the counter.
  • Listen. Hear the botanical name spoken, so you can say it out loud with confidence.

What it is not

  • Not a social network. There are no profiles, followers, or messages.
  • Not a shop. Nothing is sold inside the app and there are no in-app purchases.
  • Not a plant identifier. It does not use your camera or upload photos anywhere.
  • Not a data business. There are no accounts, no analytics and no advertising SDKs.

The cards

Photographs are of plants on the bench at work, taken by me. Card facts are checked against the RHS and the growers' own descriptions.

Privacy

Nothing leaves your phone.

Timber has no user accounts and no server of its own. Your progress, your quiz streak and your review schedule are saved in your device's local browser storage and nowhere else. Delete the app and they are gone.

The only network requests the app makes are to download its own files (the deck, the artwork, the photographs) and to keep a copy of them on your device so it works offline. It does not contact any third party, does not load advertising, and does not run analytics.

The app never asks for your camera, your location, your contacts or your microphone. The "Listen" feature uses the speech engine already on your phone and sends nothing anywhere.

Who I am

A one-man band.

My name is Oscar. I work at a garden centre in the UK, and Timber is something I built to learn the plants I sell. There is no company behind it, no investors and no team. I write the cards, take the photographs, and publish the app myself.

It started as a web page on my own phone so I could revise on the bus. Colleagues asked for it, so it became an installable app, and now it is heading to Google Play so it is easier to find and install. That is the whole story. Nothing crazy.

If you want to check anything on this page, the app's source code and every plant card in it are public on GitHub.
