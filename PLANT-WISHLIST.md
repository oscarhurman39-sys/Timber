# Plant wishlist — 50 curated additions

Fifty plants that aren't in the deck's 135 and that belong in a UK garden-centre
learning deck. Mix of genuine best-sellers, plants everyone should be able to
name, and a few that make people stop and ask.

**Status: unverified.** Every hook below is from working horticultural knowledge,
not from a source I could check — this container has no network. Treat it as a
shopping list, not as card data. Ratings, hardiness, sizes and pricing still come
from you and the label/RHS when each one goes through `PLANT-BRIEF.md`.

The deck currently holds 100 genera. Where a pick shares a genus with something
already carded it's flagged **[2nd in genus]** — worth having anyway because it's
a different plant in the eye and in the sale.

## The five you called out

| # | Plant | What people should know |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ***Tetrapanax papyrifer* 'Rex'** — rice-paper plant | The cult exotic. Dinner-plate lobed leaves to 60cm+ on a suckering stem. Hardy-ish in mild gardens (root-hardy, cut back by frost), and it **suckers a long way** — that's the thing to tell customers, not the leaves. Sold beside tree ferns and bought by the same person. |
| 2 | ***Dicksonia antarctica*** — soft tree fern | **Sold by trunk height**, and it grows only about 2.5–5cm a year, which is exactly why a 1m trunk costs what it does. Water **into the crown**, not the soil. Wrap the crown in winter. CITES Appendix II — legitimately traded with paperwork, worth knowing if anyone asks. |
| 3 | ***Rhus typhina*** — stag's horn sumach | Velvety antler-like stems, the best scarlet autumn in the trade, and it **suckers aggressively** — the single most important sentence at point of sale. 'Dissecta'/'Laciniata' gives a finer, ferny leaf. Sap can irritate. |
| 4 | ***Rodgersia podophylla* 'Bronze Peacock'** | Huge bronze palmate leaves like a horse chestnut's, holding colour through summer, cream plumes above. Moist shade or pond margin — it will scorch and crisp in dry sun. Bold texture for very little money. |
| 5 | ***Rhododendron* 'Homebush'** — deciduous azalea | The pink one. Tight rounded rose-pink semi-double trusses, Knap Hill hybrid, RHS AGM. **Deciduous azaleas are the gap in most people's knowledge** — they colour up in autumn as well as flower, and they take more sun than evergreen azaleas. [2nd in genus] |
| 5b | ***Rhododendron luteum*** — yellow azalea | Bonus, same gap: the **scented** deciduous azalea, honeysuckle-strong, AGM. If 'Homebush' sells on colour, this sells on smell. |

*Tamarix* also earns a place — see #14.

## Architectural and exotic

Your deck already leans this way (Musa, Gunnera, Fatsia, Cordyline, Yucca), so
these compound.

| # | Plant | What people should know |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | ***Trachycarpus fortunei*** — Chusan palm | **The hardiest palm you can sell for a UK garden**, genuinely H5. The one to recommend when someone asks for "a palm that survives". |
| 7 | ***Chamaerops humilis*** — dwarf fan palm | The other hardy palm, clump-forming and smaller. **Spiny leaf stalks** — position away from paths. *You already have a photo of this staged in `photos/`.* |
| 8 | ***Melianthus major*** — honey bush | Glaucous blue-grey saw-toothed leaves, and the foliage smells of **peanut butter** when brushed. A guaranteed "smell this" moment on a bench. |
| 9 | ***Astelia chathamica*** 'Silver Spear' | Metallic silver sword foliage, coastal-tough, evergreen. Reads expensive, needs sharp drainage. |
| 10 | ***Fargesia rufa*** or ***F. murielae*** — umbrella bamboo | **Clump-forming, not running.** The single most useful bamboo fact in retail: this one won't invade, *Phyllostachys* will. |
| 11 | ***Phyllostachys nigra*** — black bamboo | Sell it, but sell a root barrier with it. Canes mature to black in their second or third year, not in the pot. |
| 12 | ***Arbutus unedo*** — strawberry tree | Evergreen, and it carries **flowers and last year's fruit at the same time**. Lime-tolerant despite being in the heather family, which surprises people. |

## Shrubs — the backbone

| # | Plant | What people should know |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | ***Pieris japonica*** 'Forest Flame' | Brilliant red new growth, then cream flower panicles. **Ericaceous** — the most common reason it fails in a customer's garden. |
| 14 | ***Tamarix ramosissima*** 'Pink Cascade' | One of the few genuinely **salt-tolerant** shrubs — a real seaside answer. Feathery pink plumes late summer; prune hard or it goes leggy and bare-legged. |
| 15 | ***Skimmia japonica*** 'Rubella' | **The winter container plant.** Male, so red buds all winter but no berries — if the customer wants berries they need a female ('Nymans') plus a male. Ericaceous, hates dry. |
| 16 | ***Ceanothus*** 'Concha' — Californian lilac | The most intense blue in the trade. Say the quiet part: **short-lived and resents pruning into old wood**, wants shelter and sharp drainage. |
| 17 | ***Physocarpus opulifolius*** 'Diabolo' | Near-black foliage, pink-white flowers, tough as old boots. The dark-leaf shrub for people who kill Cotinus. |
| 18 | ***Sambucus nigra*** 'Black Lace' | Black lace-cut foliage, pink lemony flowers. Coppice it in spring for the best leaf. Reads like an acer, grows like an elder. |
| 19 | ***Syringa meyeri*** 'Palibin' — dwarf lilac | Lilac scent on a plant that actually suits a container or small garden. The answer to "I want lilac but not a 6m one". |
| 20 | ***Daphne bholua*** 'Jacqueline Postill' | Possibly the **best scent of any hardy shrub**, midwinter. And the rule: **Daphne resents disturbance** — plant it once, never move it, don't dig around it. |
| 21 | ***Sarcococca confusa*** — Christmas box | Winter scent from tiny invisible flowers, thrives in **dry shade** — the hardest brief in the garden centre. |
| 22 | ***Chaenomeles × superba*** 'Crimson and Gold' | Flowers on bare wood in late winter, then edible quinces for jelly. Wall-trainable, thorny, indestructible. |
| 23 | ***Kolkwitzia amabilis*** 'Pink Cloud' — beauty bush | Arching sprays smothered in pink bells in June. An old-fashioned plant that has quietly gone out of fashion and shouldn't have. |
| 24 | ***Deutzia gracilis*** 'Nikko' / 'Yuki Cherry Blossom' | Low mounding, drowning in white or pink stars. Genuinely low-maintenance and under-sold. |
| 25 | ***Ribes sanguineum*** 'King Edward VII' | Flowering currant — one of the earliest nectar sources for bumblebees. Foliage is pungent; some people love it, some don't. |
| 26 | ***Aucuba japonica*** 'Crotonifolia' | Gold-spotted evergreen that will grow in **deep dry shade under trees** where nothing else will. Female forms berry if a male is nearby. |
| 27 | ***Kalmia latifolia*** 'Ostbo Red' — calico bush | Crimped red buds opening to pink cups; the flowers have a **spring-loaded pollination trick** worth demonstrating. Ericaceous, slow, expensive, worth it. |
| 28 | ***Enkianthus campanulatus*** | Ericaceous, tiered branches, small waxy bells, then **one of the best autumn colours of any shrub**. A connoisseur's plant that sells itself in October. |
| 29 | ***Fothergilla major*** | Bottlebrush white flowers before the leaves, then multi-coloured autumn. Ericaceous. Compact and criminally under-known. |
| 30 | ***Hypericum × inodorum*** MAGICAL series | Grown for **berries**, not flowers — bronze, red or white, and a florist staple. Reframe it as a cut-stem crop and it sells differently. |

## Trees and small trees

| # | Plant | What people should know |
|---|---|---|
| 31 | ***Betula utilis* var. *jacquemontii*** 'Doorenbos' | The whitest bark of any tree. Bark **whitens with age** — a young plant in a pot isn't lying, it just hasn't got there yet. Wash the trunk to keep it bright. |
| 32 | ***Amelanchier lamarckii*** — snowy mespilus | Arguably **the best small garden tree**: white blossom, bronze new leaf, edible berries, fiery autumn. Four seasons on one plant. |
| 33 | ***Parrotia persica*** — Persian ironwood | Witch-hazel relative, spidery red flowers on bare wood, then the best autumn colour in the business. Lime-tolerant, unlike most autumn stars. |
| 34 | ***Nyssa sylvatica*** 'Wisley Bonfire' — tupelo | Autumn colour that stops traffic. Needs **moist, acid, non-limey** soil — say so, or it's a returned plant. |
| 35 | ***Acer griseum*** — paperbark maple | Cinnamon bark peeling in translucent curls, best in winter sun. **Slow**, which is exactly why it's priced as it is. [2nd in genus] |
| 36 | ***Corylus avellana*** 'Contorta' — corkscrew hazel | Twisted stems for winter structure and flower arranging, catkins in February. Usually **grafted** — remove straight suckers from the base or it reverts. |
| 37 | ***Malus*** 'Evereste' — crab apple | AGM, brilliant blossom, orange-red fruit that hangs for months, and a **universal pollination partner for eating apples** — the sales angle most people miss. |
| 38 | ***Koelreuteria paniculata*** — golden rain tree | Yellow panicles in high summer when nothing else flowers, then inflated papery lanterns. Loves a hot dry spot. |
| 39 | ***Gleditsia triacanthos*** 'Sunburst' — honey locust | Golden ferny foliage casting **light dappled shade you can plant under**. Thornless form, tolerates urban air. |
| 40 | ***Eucalyptus gunnii*** 'Azura' | Compact cider gum bred for gardens rather than forestry. Silver-blue, evergreen, cut-foliage crop. Coppice to keep juvenile leaves. |

## Climbers

| # | Plant | What people should know |
|---|---|---|
| 41 | ***Wisteria floribunda*** 'Multijuga' / *sinensis* 'Prolific' | **Buy grafted, never seed-raised** — a seedling can take 20 years to flower. Two prunes a year: July to five buds, February to two or three. The most valuable knowledge in the whole climber bay. |
| 42 | ***Trachelospermum jasminoides*** — star jasmine | Evergreen, glossy, powerfully scented white pinwheels; foliage bronzes in cold. **The default answer for an evergreen scented climber.** *Was flagged as a photo gap in an earlier session.* |
| 43 | ***Hedera colchica*** 'Sulphur Heart' — Persian ivy | Huge splashed gold leaves, self-clinging, shade-proof. Ivy's reputation is worse than the plant. |
| 44 | ***Akebia quinata*** — chocolate vine | Vanilla-chocolate-scented maroon flowers in spring. Needs **two plants for fruit**, and the fruit is bizarre enough to be a talking point. |
| 45 | ***Lonicera periclymenum*** 'Serotina' — late Dutch honeysuckle | Scent strongest at **dusk**, because it's moth-pollinated. Explains itself once you say it. [2nd in genus] |

## Small hardy plants — the Bridge Nursery end

| # | Plant | What people should know |
|---|---|---|
| 46 | ***Geranium*** 'Rozanne' | **The best-selling hardy perennial there is** — RHS Plant of the Centenary. Flowers June to first frost, sterile so it never stops trying to set seed. If one perennial belongs in the deck, it's this. |
| 47 | ***Erysimum*** 'Bowles's Mauve' | Flowers ten months of the year. Also **short-lived — 2 or 3 years** — so take cuttings. Tell people that up front and they'll forgive it. |
| 48 | ***Nepeta racemosa*** 'Walker's Low' | Catmint. Bees adore it, cats roll on it, and the Chelsea chop in late May keeps it upright instead of splayed. |
| 49 | ***Helleborus × hybridus*** / 'Anna's Red' | Winter flowers in shade. **Cut the old leaves off in January** — it shows the flowers and removes leaf-spot. |
| 50 | ***Hakonechloa macra*** 'Aureola' — Japanese forest grass | AGM. The one ornamental grass that genuinely prefers **shade** and moisture, flowing like water in a pot. Deciduous, late to appear in spring — don't let anyone dig it up thinking it's dead. |
